Speaker notes (~3 minutes)

Target: 400–450 words · ~130 wpm · Practice with a timer

0:00–0:30 Hook & problem

Imagine you have just had liver transplant surgery. You are sent home with instructions — but who checks your wound every day? Complications like infection or poor healing often show visual signs before patients realise something is wrong. In low-resource settings, follow-up visits are limited, and clinicians cannot manually review hundreds of wound photos. That gap between discharge and the next clinic visit is where PostCare AI operates.

0:30–1:30 What we built

PostCare is a multi-agent post-operative care platform. The patient opens a mobile web app, uploads a daily wound photo, and logs pain and symptoms. Our fine-tuned MedSigLIP model — trained on the SurgWound dataset with a documented Run 1 to Run 2 MLOps pipeline — assesses six clinical visual signals: healing status, erythema, edema, infection risk, urgency, and exudate.

Deterministic safety rules flag high pain or concerning visual scores. Then three AI agents run in sequence: triage sets priority, a patient agent delivers plain-language guidance, and a clinician agent prepares a structured handoff summary. Patients can also chat with a RAG recovery assistant that retrieves from a vector knowledge base aligned with their personal recovery data.

1:30–2:30 Why it matters

This is not autonomous diagnosis — it is decision support with humans in the loop. High-priority cases appear on a clinician dashboard at PKLI Lahore, where a surgeon or nurse can review and mark cases complete. We combine computer vision, retrieval-augmented generation, and LLM agents into one pipeline — from Kaggle training to a production FastAPI backend with patient and clinician frontends.

The MLOps story matters: Run 1 intentionally underfit to establish a baseline; Run 2 improved with differential learning rates and per-label Youden thresholds — showing iterative, evidence-based model development, not a single black-box training run.

2:30–3:00 Closing

PostCare AI aims to make post-operative wound monitoring scalable, earlier, and safer — giving patients daily reassurance and giving clinicians a prioritised queue of who needs attention most. Our next step is clinical validation with qualified wound-care specialists. Thank you.

Anticipated questions

  • Is it FDA approved? No — research prototype / capstone.
  • Why MedSigLIP? Medical-domain vision-language model; fine-tuned for surgical wounds.
  • What if the model is wrong? Clinician review + safety flags; not a replacement for care teams.
